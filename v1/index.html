Technical University of Denmark (DTU) · Roskilde, Denmark

Studying the impact of electric vehicles on low-voltage distribution grids—transforming EVs from a burden into a flexibility asset.

My research addresses the physical and operational bottlenecks of mass EV adoption on distribution networks within the DTEC GREAT and EV4EU initiatives. I analyze and develop model-free, decentralized charging strategies that respect realistic communication constraints, enable EVs to provide frequency regulation to TSOs without causing low-voltage congestion, and evaluate system impacts using stochastic Monte Carlo simulations.

Research Pillars

01

Model-Free & Decentralized Charging

Analyzing and developing model-free, decentralized charging strategies considering realistic communication between chargers and aggregators.

02

TSO Frequency Services without LV Issues

Allowing EVs to provide frequency ancillary services to transmission system operators (TSOs) without causing thermal overloads or voltage violations at the low-voltage level where most EVs are connected.

03

Monte Carlo Simulations

Assessing hosting capacity and charging dynamics via Monte Carlo simulations that capture the natural stochasticity of EV user behavior and charging.
